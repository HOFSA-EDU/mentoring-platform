# Product Vision

## Problem

First-semester BTS students often communicate difficulties too late.

The current mentoring model depends heavily on the student recognizing a problem and actively telling the mentor about it.

In practice, this often happens late.

Traditional tools such as SWOT analysis are also limited in this context because they require students to already understand:

- their strengths
- their weaknesses
- relevant risks
- opportunities
- their own behavioural patterns

Many new students do not yet have enough experience or reflection ability to answer these questions meaningfully.

The result is often generic self-assessment with little practical value.

---

## Product hypothesis

Concrete recurring questions are easier to answer than abstract self-assessment.

Instead of asking:

> What are your weaknesses?

the platform asks questions such as:

> How often did you start an assignment later than you originally intended this week?

or:

> When you did not understand something, how quickly did you ask for help?

Repeated answers over time make patterns visible.

Patterns create better mentor conversations.

Better conversations allow earlier support.

---

## Product objective

The platform should help students develop self-awareness while helping mentors identify emerging difficulties earlier.

It should transform mentoring from:

> Student has a problem → student eventually tells mentor

into:

> Student reflects regularly → patterns become visible → mentor receives a concrete conversation trigger → student and mentor agree on a small action → progress is reviewed

---

## Primary product loop

CHECK-IN  
→ PATTERN  
→ OBSERVATION  
→ MENTOR CONVERSATION  
→ AGREED ACTION  
→ QUEST  
→ FOLLOW-UP  
→ REFLECTION  
→ NEXT CHECK-IN

This loop is the core of the product.

---

## Target users

### Primary

First-semester BTS students.

Typical age:

approximately 17–21 years.

### Secondary

Mentors supporting those students during their studies.

### Administrative

BTS coordinators or administrators managing:

- mentor assignments
- semesters
- questions
- Quest templates

---

## Product principles

### 1. Reflection before diagnosis

The platform helps students observe themselves.

It does not diagnose students.

### 2. Behaviour before labels

Prefer:

> You reported difficulty starting tasks during the last three weeks.

Avoid:

> You are unmotivated.

### 3. Trends before snapshots

One difficult week does not define a student.

Repeated or changing patterns matter more.

### 4. Student ownership

Students must benefit directly from their own data.

The platform must not become a staff surveillance dashboard.

### 5. Human mentoring remains central

The system supports mentor conversations.

It does not replace them.

### 6. Small actions

When a pattern is identified, the response should usually be a small practical action rather than a large intervention.

### 7. Explainability

Every system-generated observation must be traceable to concrete student responses.

### 8. Privacy by design

The platform may contain sensitive educational and personal information.

Collect only what is necessary.

Restrict access by role.

---

## What success looks like

A successful interaction might look like this:

1. A student completes several weekly check-ins.
2. The student repeatedly reports difficulty starting assignments.
3. The system creates a factual observation.
4. The mentor sees the observation before the student reaches a crisis point.
5. Mentor and student discuss the pattern.
6. They agree on a small experiment: start one assignment three days early for 15 minutes.
7. The Quest is assigned.
8. The student tries it.
9. The student reflects on whether it helped.
10. Future check-ins show whether the pattern changed.

---

## Primary pilot question

The MVP should answer:

> Did the platform trigger useful mentoring conversations that otherwise would probably have happened later?

This matters more than:

- number of badges earned
- number of page views
- number of features
- total time spent in the application

---

## What the product is not

The platform is not:

- an attendance system
- a grading system
- a class register
- a psychological assessment
- a diagnostic system
- a dropout prediction engine
- a parent portal
- a social network
- a chat application
- a replacement for professional psychological support
- a replacement for mentors

Attendance is already handled elsewhere and must not be duplicated.

---

## Gamification philosophy

Gamification exists to increase engagement and make personal development visible.

It should feel subtle and appropriate for young adults.

Useful mechanisms include:

- semester journey
- Quests
- milestones
- progress indicators
- personal development over time

Avoid:

- leaderboards
- public ranking
- competitive XP
- virtual currencies
- childish graphics
- excessive badges

The student should feel:

> I can see how I am developing.

not:

> I am playing a school game.

---

## Long-term potential

The MVP should remain narrow.

Potential future extensions may include:

- teacher observations
- additional mentoring workflows
- cohort-level anonymized insights
- configurable programs
- additional question libraries
- richer student reflection tools

These are not part of the initial MVP unless explicitly added later.

---

## Core product statement

We do not ask students to already know who they are.

We help them recognize patterns in what they actually experience and do.

Those patterns create better conversations.

Better conversations enable earlier support.
